## 5. Payroll and Take-Home-Pay Calculations

### Step 1 — Income normalization

Normalize the selected input period into an annual calculation basis.

Use the verified project conversion factors.

### Step 2 — Federal income tax

Use official IRS tax rules applicable to the selected year.

Verify:

- Tax brackets.
- Filing statuses.
- Standard deductions.
- Applicable itemized deduction rules.
- Relevant credits and eligibility limitations.
- Additional taxes where applicable.

Apply progressive tax brackets rather than a flat bracket percentage.

Do not invent official figures.

### Step 3 — State and local income taxes

Determine the authorized state and local jurisdiction.

Apply official income-tax rules only when required by the verified specification.

Handle:

- States without a general individual income tax.
- Flat-rate states.
- Progressive-tax states.
- Applicable local taxes.
- State-specific deductions and exemptions.

Do not assume the county dropdown alone is sufficient to determine all state and local income taxes.

### Step 4 — Social Security and Medicare

Where actual taxable payroll wages and applicable employment information are available, use official SSA/IRS rules for the selected year.

Account for:

- Social Security taxable wage base.
- Employee Social Security rate.
- Medicare rate.
- Additional Medicare Tax where applicable.
- Relevant wage and employment distinctions.

If the screenshot's AGI-only inputs cannot support accurate payroll-tax computation, do not present an exact calculated FICA amount.

### Step 5 — Post-tax deductions

Subtract verified post-tax deductions from the corresponding annual or periodic net paycheck basis.

Avoid treating them as income-tax deductions.

### Step 6 — Property tax treatment

**CRITICAL: Property tax is not automatically a payroll withholding.**

Keep the property-tax estimate conceptually separate from statutory payroll withholding.

Only incorporate it into a broader disposable-income or after-housing-tax estimate if the original authorized tool specification explicitly requires that treatment.

If included in such an estimate, label it clearly so users do not mistake the result for actual employer-issued take-home pay.

### Step 7 — Take-home-pay estimation

When all required payroll inputs are available and sufficiently defined:

`Annual Take-Home Pay = Annual Gross Payroll Wages − Applicable Payroll Withholdings − Applicable Income-Tax Withholdings − Post-Tax Payroll Deductions`

Convert annual net pay into the selected pay frequency.

Tax liability, paycheck withholding, and year-end net income are not necessarily identical.

If the authorized tool only provides AGI, do not silently treat it as gross payroll wages to produce a falsely precise paycheck.

Use existing verified project estimation conventions, explanatory limitations, or unavailable states.
