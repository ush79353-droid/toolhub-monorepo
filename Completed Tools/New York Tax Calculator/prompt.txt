### 5. Critical AGI Interpretation

The screenshot explicitly requests **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)**.

AGI is not necessarily equal to:

- Gross salary.
- Gross payroll compensation.
- Social Security taxable wages.
- Medicare wages.
- New York State adjusted gross income.
- New York City taxable income.

Therefore:

- Use federal AGI as the starting point for the applicable simplified federal calculation.
- Do not automatically assume federal AGI equals New York State AGI.
- Apply New York-specific adjustments only where supported by the available information.
- Clearly disclose when the calculator assumes no additional New York modifications.

**Do not calculate FICA as an exact percentage of AGI.**

Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes require wage or self-employment information that the screenshot does not provide.

If the original specification expects a full paycheck calculation, distinguish actual wage-based payroll tax calculations from an AGI-based income tax estimate.

Do not fabricate precise take-home pay.

### 6. Federal Income Tax Calculation

For a supported filing status and tax year:

1. Start with annualized federal AGI.
2. Determine the applicable verified federal standard deduction.
3. Compare standard deduction with the entered itemized amount where appropriate.
4. Apply the valid federal deduction.
5. Calculate taxable income.
6. Apply the official progressive tax brackets.
7. Apply only supported tax credits and adjustments.

General simplified calculation:

`Federal Taxable Income = max(0, AGI − Applicable Federal Deduction)`

`Federal Income Tax = ProgressiveTax(Federal Taxable Income, Federal Brackets)`

Important:

- Do not multiply the entire taxable income by its highest marginal rate.
- Do not automatically assign a fixed tax credit per dependent without sufficient eligibility data.
- Do not assume AGI contains only ordinary income if the calculation is presented as an exact federal tax liability.
- Explain that capital gains, qualified dividends and other tax attributes may require additional information.

### 9. Pay Frequency and Period Conversion

Use the confirmed pay frequency to present annual estimates per pay period.

Standard periods:

- Weekly: `52`.
- Biweekly: `26`.
- Semimonthly: `24`.
- Monthly: `12`.

Where supported:

`Estimated Federal Tax Per Period = Annual Federal Tax / Periods Per Year`

`Estimated NY Tax Per Period = Annual NY Tax / Periods Per Year`

`Estimated Post-Tax Income = Estimated Income After Income Taxes − Applicable Post-Tax Deductions`

- Verify whether the post-tax deduction is annual or per pay period.
- Do not apply it twice.
- Do not subtract it from federal, state or NYC taxable income.
- Validate nonnegative values.
- If the user's deduction period cannot be established, explain the assumption and avoid unsupported exact paycheck claims.
