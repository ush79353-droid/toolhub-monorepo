## 4. Lump-Sum Calculation

For:

- `A` = advertised annuity jackpot amount.
- `p` = lump-sum payout percentage.

Calculate:

`Lump Sum Payout = A × p / 100`

Example:

`1,200,000,000 × 52 / 100 = 624,000,000`

Validate payout percentage within 0–100%.

Preserve full numerical precision and round only for presentation.

## 5. Customized Tax Calculations

Let:

- `T1` = first tax percentage.
- `T2` = second tax percentage.

For independent taxes applied to the same gross taxable payout:

`Tax I = Gross Payout × T1 / 100`

`Tax II = Gross Payout × T2 / 100`

`Net Payout = Gross Payout − Tax I − Tax II`

Requirements:

- Do not compound Tax II on a Tax-I-reduced base unless the verified specification explicitly requires it.
- Validate nonnegative tax rates.
- Prevent combined deductions from silently producing an impossible negative payout.
- Clearly indicate when custom rates are illustrative rather than official tax liability.
- Apply custom rates consistently to each annual annuity payment.
- Never treat arbitrary tax percentages as official US tax rates.

## 6. US Lottery Tax Treatment

Verify applicable rules using authoritative sources:

- IRS.
- Relevant US state revenue departments.
- Official lottery operator payout rules where needed.

Research and implement only the rules justified by the available inputs.

Important distinctions:

- Federal withholding is not necessarily the final federal tax liability.
- Lottery winnings generally constitute taxable income under applicable federal rules.
- Actual federal liability may depend on other income, filing status, deductions and tax year.
- State taxation depends on the applicable jurisdiction and lottery-specific rules.
- Lottery prizes may involve nonresident withholding considerations.
- Annuity installments can be taxed in the year received.

**Never apply a federal withholding percentage and label it as exact final tax liability.**

If the US tax mode lacks required details such as state or filing status, use clearly labeled estimates and explain the limitations, or provide an unavailable state where a reliable calculation cannot be performed.

Verify 2026 or the applicable selected-year rules before using them.

Do not invent state-specific rates.

Do not automatically assume the entire lottery award is taxable in one year when paid as an annuity.
